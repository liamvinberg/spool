# spool canvas

This folder is a [spool](https://spool.page) project: live TSX frames on an infinite canvas — agents author the files, humans arrange and play them.

Run `pnpm dev skill` before working here — this repo's checkout CLI, never the installed `spool`. It is the complete contract: if it isn't in there, spool doesn't do it. Topics: `pnpm dev skill frames|flows|scenarios|mock|styling|verbs`.

- A frame is born by writing `frames/<page>/<name>/frame.tsx` default-exporting one React component — no registration, no `spool new`. Variants are `--`-named siblings (`checkout--empty/`).
- The one law: never write app-owned files — `canvas.json` and `.spool/` are spool's.
- Commit completed design work atomically before handoff.

## Voice

How spool talks, wherever words face a user — frames, chrome, the site, docs.

- One rule, two registers. If the machine would print it, it is verbatim lowercase mono: commands, paths, frame names, chips, counts, status lines (`live · esc exits`, `no frames yet`). If a person is saying it, it is a sentence: sentence case, proper nouns restored (Node, Chrome, TSX, GitHub), a period when it is a whole sentence.
- The name is "spool" in every register, sentence start included — wordmark, command and prose share the one form.
- Plain declaratives that carry their own proof ("It feels real because it is."). No hype adjectives, no exclamation marks, no em-dashes in copy.
- When the terminal is the subject, let it speak: the prompt names the working directory rather than a sentence saying "in your repo".
- A demo product on a frame speaks like a real product — its own name, its own sentence case — never in spool's costume.

What ships reads the backlog instead (`min(83 c/s, 250ms ÷ pending)`, closed-form in **`shared/lib/say-pace.ts`**, assistant-ui's `useSmooth` mechanism retuned because its own 200 c/s floor is faster than Opus 5 writes). It costs 0.8s of lag and finishes 0.26s after the last delta. `fade` at 170ms ships over `blur` and `soften`, which Chromium refuses to composite, and over `edge`, which *freezes* mid-gradient when the wire pauses. The caret stays, always on and **static**, because every fade completes during a pause and it is then the only thing saying more is coming — but nothing that ships blinks one: of thirteen chat surfaces read at the source, zero blink at the live edge, the two that pair a caret with a per-word fade both draw a static glyph, and WCAG 2.2.2 names blinking under the same five-second trigger as motion. It is one `Caret` in `spool-say.tsx` now rather than three copies. Three further things landed out of those sheets: the markdown renderer is **`shared/ui/spool-say.tsx`**, used by the rail and both sheets, over **`shared/lib/say-markers.ts`**, which **closes a marker the message has not finished writing** — a partial marker is a *block*, so `\n\n*` is a paragraph the next character deletes, and holding markers back pumped one in and out six times per message; closing them instead means the finished text is byte-identical to what was already drawn, measured at zero height decreases across all three messages, and #149 added the last rule it was missing — a lone `-`, `*` or `1.` waits for its space, which takes the *rendered prefix* breaks across all 5,808 streamed characters from 6 to **0** and is what makes an animate-on-mount arrival safe at all; **the transcript anchors the top of a live entry rather than the bottom of the log** whenever that entry is taller than the box; and that anchoring **exonerated #145's reserve** — the empty-screen argument against it was a bottom-pinning artefact, and what is left against it is only that it puts screens of scrollable nothing into the log. **#161 resolved: a thread waiting on a person is its own reading of the mark, and it is `unread`'s disc held inside `working`'s ring.** `ThreadMark` carries three readings now rather than two, and `Life` gained `waiting`, which `PlayRail` hands to the strip because a parked turn never leaves `playing` — `useTurn` schedules nothing past the hold, so the phase alone drew a turning ring for a thread that had stopped and was burning nothing. The losers are on the sheet `agent-nav-marks--held`, a sibling of `agent-nav-marks` because the object is not the same: that sheet decided the *nav cell*, which #144 then deleted with the whole tab row, while what ships is a bare 14px box with no glyph in it. Two candidates died on facts rather than taste and both are drawn: freezing the spinner is pixel-identical to what `prefers-reduced-motion` already renders for *working*, and borrowing the unread disc breaks on the clearing rule, since a disc clears when you open a thread and a question does not. So the winner had to differ from both in **shape**, and being the loudest of the three is right rather than a cost — it is the only one of them that is actually stuck. #127's signed-out bounce is the same state and shares the mark; #122's wind-down is not, because the agent is told to finish and does. **#163 resolved: a settled message leaves nothing behind, and the span that was there was a defect rather than a cost.** `agent-say-settle` draws four real messages seven ways across twelve column widths and measures every word with a `Range`, which turned #148's stated reason for wrapping every word inside out: `inline-block whitespace-pre` is `text-wrap: nowrap` inside the box, so a wrapped word **cannot break inside itself**, and this corpus is made of words that should — `'kaffe-receipt-copy'`, `finance/service-design`, `design/shared/tokens.css`. It broke the prose differently from raw text at **3, 8 and 9 of 12 widths, the rail's own 392px included**. Height and line count never moved, which is why looking at a total never caught it; the reflow is lateral. `inline-block` was doing nothing — `white-space` alone diverges at the same widths — and a plain span diverges at 0, 2 and 1, never at 392px, splitting the same word raw splits. So `WORD` is gone from **`shared/ui/spool-say.tsx`**: an arriving word gets a plain span, a settled word gets no element at all, and `Said` settled now measures **0 elements from raw text's own DOM** (74 against `block`'s 633 on the long message) with the live window worth 27 spans. Nothing is unwrapped at a finish line because the window was the only thing wrapped. Two things fell out: `nodes`, a column with no elements in it, diverges at exactly the same widths as `inline`, so the residual is **per-word tokenisation itself** rather than the span, and it is the floor for any per-word animation; and the cost was never the reason — the four archived sessions hold 35 messages and 1,784 words of prose all told, largest thread 836 — but `railEntries()` rebuilds every entry on every tick and `Entry` is not memoised, so a settled message's tree is re-created while the next one streams, now 8.5× smaller. Unmeasured and stated: a plain inline span has no box, so its opacity animation is likelier to run on the main thread than the compositor. **#165 resolved: a turn in flight is stopped by a press in the composer footer and by esc, and what it catches is neither done nor failed.** `agent-stop` is canonical and plays `claude-interrupt.json`, the sixth fixture and one no frame had ever opened; `--field` and `--edge` are the placements it beat — the composer box, where every other chat surface puts it, loses because Spool **has no send button to morph** (Enter sends), so it would be adding the slot it deliberately lacks and leaving it empty whenever no turn runs; the live edge loses because it *travels*, fastest exactly when rows are piling up, and scrolls away the moment you read back. **Esc was never spent**: `canvas.tsx:2553` is a ladder of eight meanings and #139 owns one rung, and `canvas.tsx:2347` ignores every key while focus is in a textarea — so esc in the composer had been going nowhere at the precise moment a turn is running. The binary does both halves too (`press Esc to stop` with a terminal, `press Ctrl+C to stop` without). The turn player gained a **cut** to go with #145's `hold`: a hold parks on a cue the script names, a cut lands wherever the hand does, so `TurnPhase` has a fourth member and `useTurn` takes a second optional cue for the capture's own stop — leave the frame alone and it still ends at 17.9s the way the recording did. The aftermath is **derived rather than projected**, which is the only way a press at an arbitrary second leaves what the recording left: every row still open when the clock stopped never finished. `RowState` has a fifth member drawn as **one flat stroke** — a check is two strokes meeting, a cross two crossing — and it is needed because the wire conflates the cases: an interrupt stamps the call it caught `toolDenialKind: "user-rejected"`, the identical value a permission decline gets, so forking on `is_error` alone draws #142's cross and says a `read` failed when it never ran. A row cut mid-argument draws as **a bare verb with no subject**, which needed no special case — it is beat one of the three every tool call already gets. `[Request interrupted by user]` is **not drawn**: it is addressed to the model so its next turn knows why the work ends, and echoing it reports your own press back at you wearing the 2px rail the transcript gives *your* words; Spool says `stopped` as a rule instead. Nothing is torn on disk — the interrupt is a control request, not a kill — and the thread carries no mark, on #161's own test. **#162's dismiss is finally drawn**, as `agent-play--ask-deny`: three full-width bordered options and then one quiet mono word, which must not read as a fourth because an option is an answer and this is the refusal of the whole question. It shares #165's mark, since a deny also means the tool never ran, while `nobody answered` keeps the cross as its opposite. The frame #165 asked for — both exits together — does not exist and could not: they cannot co-occur (`phase === "playing" && !waiting`), they sit in different halves of the rail, and no capture holds both. **#176 resolved: a queued message never leaves the composer.** It stacks inside the composer's own border, above #116's chip strip and the field, dimmed with a mono `queued` and a ✕ — so firing is the send it already is (the stack leaves the box and lands in the log, the journey every message makes), take-back is a drop into the field it is sitting on, and the stack is what #170 settled fires as *one* turn. `agent-play--queue-box` is canonical and `--queue-back` is the gesture, opening mid-sentence so the merge has something to merge with. The three losers are drawn: `--queue-tail` puts the rows at the end of the log, `--queue-band` on a ruled strip between log and composer, `--queue-strip` on one line with a count. **Tail and band turned out to be one design** — the transcript auto-follows, so while you are watching, the tail's queue sits exactly where the band puts it, and they diverge only once you scroll up. Neither original frame could show that, or anything else: both were parked mid-turn forever, so **the moment they disagree about — firing — never happened**, and both played `claude-turn.json`'s eight-second window, so the log never filled. All five now play `claude-plan.json` and run to their end, and the queue fires on `settled` into ordinary user rows. The split click #143 set a precedent for is **dead by #170's own invariant**: *words that leave the queue un-fired land back in the box* leaves one destination, and a split click needs two. Returned words land **above** the draft with a blank line, because the queue's order is the order they were going to be said in and the caret is mid-word; the cost is that two messages coming back become one blob, which the blank line leaves splittable. `useQueue` in **`shared/lib/agent-queue.ts`** owns the list, the fire and the merge; the composer is controlled only when `draft`/`onDraft` are both handed in, which nothing before take-back needed. `app` is the baseline. A new prototype starts by copying the frame it changes,
not from nothing — so the thing being proposed is legible as a diff against
what exists.

Keeping it honest is the whole job. Every frame on `app` must match the code
in `src/ui/` and `src/runtime/`; when a design ships, the frame here becomes
what shipped. Read the implementation before trusting a frame — that is how
"design mode" survived here for months after select became the only pointer
tool. What the chrome is today: one 44px bar (brand lockup, project tabs, "+",
then threads toggle and zoom), the Pages rail at 248px, the Inspector rail at
300px with its elements and connections tabs, and the tool bar floating over
the bottom of the viewport. No mode switch. Play lives on the selection.

Explorations live until the work they decided is built. Making the decision is
not that moment: while a page is still being resolved, the rejected frames are
what the next session reads to see what was already argued and why, and a
component only the loser references dies with it. When the work ships, the
winner moves onto `app` as what shipped and the rest are deleted then. Git
history is the archive, and `git log --diff-filter=D --stat -- design/frames`
finds them.
