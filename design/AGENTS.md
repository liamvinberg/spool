# spool canvas

This folder is a [spool](https://spool.page) project: live TSX frames on an infinite canvas — agents author the files, humans arrange and play them.

Run `pnpm dev skill` before working here — this repo's checkout CLI, never the installed `spool`. It is the complete contract: if it isn't in there, spool doesn't do it. Topics: `pnpm dev skill frames|flows|scenarios|mock|styling|verbs`.

- A frame is born by writing `frames/<page>/<name>/frame.tsx` default-exporting one React component — no registration, no `spool new`. Variants are `--`-named siblings (`checkout--empty/`).
- The one law: never write app-owned files — `canvas.json` and `.spool/` are spool's.
- Commit completed design work atomically before handoff.

## The pages

Every frame lives on a page; the root page stays empty on purpose.

| Page | What it holds |

| `agent` | The agent chat (#114), unbuilt and still being decided. A wayfinder map is resolving it one ticket at a time, so the page holds rejected variants next to canonical ones on purpose. Canonical so far: `agent-play--plan-pinned`, `agent-play--shot-open`, `agent-play--model-menu`, `agent-play--limit-line`, `agent-play--limit-stop`, `agent-play--threads-strip`, `agent-play--edit-run`, `agent-play--wall-install`, `agent-play--wall-login`, `agent-play--entered`, `agent-play--jump-name`, `agent-play--mcp-ask`, `agent-play--say-read`, `agent-say-pace`. **#144 removed the tab row from every frame here**: the agent owns the rail whole, so `PlayRail` draws #136's threads strip over the transcript instead — derived from the one turn a frame plays, unless the frame hands it a real deck. The plus leads that row and a press centres the row on what was pressed. Two sheets rather than screens carry the losing candidates and the reasoning: `agent-nav-marks` for the glyphs and where a state lives, `agent-nav-strip` for the overflow. **#146 resolved: the flow map draws everything it knows, always** — off-page and broken walks dock on their declaring frame in the coaching layer's language, one toggle (re-iconed to the `edge` glyph) hides the whole layer, `verified` gets no face. Canonical: `agent-walk-ambient`, `--off`, `--dense`; the seven rejected directions live in the rows above them (`agent-mark-*` and the `agent-walk-*` families) until #151 builds it. **#145's row is the first one you have to answer**: `--ask-log`, `--ask-composer` and `--ask-shelf` are the three places a question's options can go, and `--ask-drop` is the same capture with the clock left running, which is the only one that shows the agent answering for you. They are also the first frames whose turn *stops* — `useTurn` takes a `hold` cue and parks on it, because the capture had no client and so its question was live for 84ms. **#148 resolved: an agent message renders whole and nothing is clamped, and its row is the first one that renders markdown**: `--say-raw` is what the rail did until now, source markers and all, kept as the diff; `--say-read`, `--say-lede` and `--say-lines` are the three answers to how much room a 3,372-character message may take. They play a new `say` slice of `claude-mcp.json` — every other window in `claude-turn.ts` ends before the long message, which is why the question had never been drawn. `--say-read` is canonical. How the live edge *looks* while it arrives spun off as [#149](https://github.com/liamvinberg/spool/issues/149), **now resolved: the backlog sets the rate, and a word fades in on arrival.** Four sheets carry it, all on the model of `agent-nav-strip`, all rendered and all reading from the top. `agent-say-stream` compares ways of arranging the wire's own steps and finds none of them smooth, because the step *is* the problem: a delta carries a median of 81 characters, so the block gains one to two whole lines at once. `agent-say-arrive` compares the live edge itself (`fade`, `blur`, `edge`, `soften`) over all three streamed messages the repo has. **The unit is a word, not a character**: a per-glyph span breaks text shaping at its own boundary, so glyphs leaving the live window changed the paragraph's width every frame and the block read as flickering. `agent-say-pace` and `--jitter` are #149's own, and they are the canonical pair: three pace policies with a live rate trace above each, on the interpolated 460ms and then on gaps swinging 150ms to 1.2s. **The jitter frame is the argument** — spending a chunk across a fixed beat, which is what the first two sheets did, dumps its unspent remainder in one frame at 5,520 c/s when the next chunk lands early, and freezes when it lands late. What ships reads the backlog instead (`min(83 c/s, 250ms ÷ pending)`, closed-form in **`shared/lib/say-pace.ts`**, assistant-ui's `useSmooth` mechanism retuned because its own 200 c/s floor is faster than Opus 5 writes). It costs 0.8s of lag and finishes 0.26s after the last delta. `fade` at 170ms ships over `blur` and `soften`, which Chromium refuses to composite, and over `edge`, which *freezes* mid-gradient when the wire pauses. The caret stays, always on and **static**, because every fade completes during a pause and it is then the only thing saying more is coming — but nothing that ships blinks one: of thirteen chat surfaces read at the source, zero blink at the live edge, the two that pair a caret with a per-word fade both draw a static glyph, and WCAG 2.2.2 names blinking under the same five-second trigger as motion. It is one `Caret` in `spool-say.tsx` now rather than three copies. Three further things landed out of those sheets: the markdown renderer is **`shared/ui/spool-say.tsx`**, used by the rail and both sheets, over **`shared/lib/say-markers.ts`**, which **closes a marker the message has not finished writing** — a partial marker is a *block*, so `\n\n*` is a paragraph the next character deletes, and holding markers back pumped one in and out six times per message; closing them instead means the finished text is byte-identical to what was already drawn, measured at zero height decreases across all three messages, and #149 added the last rule it was missing — a lone `-`, `*` or `1.` waits for its space, which takes the *rendered prefix* breaks across all 5,808 streamed characters from 6 to **0** and is what makes an animate-on-mount arrival safe at all; **the transcript anchors the top of a live entry rather than the bottom of the log** whenever that entry is taller than the box; and that anchoring **exonerated #145's reserve** — the empty-screen argument against it was a bottom-pinning artefact, and what is left against it is only that it puts screens of scrollable nothing into the log. **#161 resolved: a thread waiting on a person is its own reading of the mark, and it is `unread`'s disc held inside `working`'s ring.** `ThreadMark` carries three readings now rather than two, and `Life` gained `waiting`, which `PlayRail` hands to the strip because a parked turn never leaves `playing` — `useTurn` schedules nothing past the hold, so the phase alone drew a turning ring for a thread that had stopped and was burning nothing. The losers are on the sheet `agent-nav-marks--held`, a sibling of `agent-nav-marks` because the object is not the same: that sheet decided the *nav cell*, which #144 then deleted with the whole tab row, while what ships is a bare 14px box with no glyph in it. Two candidates died on facts rather than taste and both are drawn: freezing the spinner is pixel-identical to what `prefers-reduced-motion` already renders for *working*, and borrowing the unread disc breaks on the clearing rule, since a disc clears when you open a thread and a question does not. So the winner had to differ from both in **shape**, and being the loudest of the three is right rather than a cost — it is the only one of them that is actually stuck. #127's signed-out bounce is the same state and shares the mark; #122's wind-down is not, because the agent is told to finish and does. **#163 resolved: a settled message leaves nothing behind, and the span that was there was a defect rather than a cost.** `agent-say-settle` draws four real messages seven ways across twelve column widths and measures every word with a `Range`, which turned #148's stated reason for wrapping every word inside out: `inline-block whitespace-pre` is `text-wrap: nowrap` inside the box, so a wrapped word **cannot break inside itself**, and this corpus is made of words that should — `'kaffe-receipt-copy'`, `finance/service-design`, `design/shared/tokens.css`. It broke the prose differently from raw text at **3, 8 and 9 of 12 widths, the rail's own 392px included**. Height and line count never moved, which is why looking at a total never caught it; the reflow is lateral. `inline-block` was doing nothing — `white-space` alone diverges at the same widths — and a plain span diverges at 0, 2 and 1, never at 392px, splitting the same word raw splits. So `WORD` is gone from **`shared/ui/spool-say.tsx`**: an arriving word gets a plain span, a settled word gets no element at all, and `Said` settled now measures **0 elements from raw text's own DOM** (74 against `block`'s 633 on the long message) with the live window worth 27 spans. Nothing is unwrapped at a finish line because the window was the only thing wrapped. Two things fell out: `nodes`, a column with no elements in it, diverges at exactly the same widths as `inline`, so the residual is **per-word tokenisation itself** rather than the span, and it is the floor for any per-word animation; and the cost was never the reason — the four archived sessions hold 35 messages and 1,784 words of prose all told, largest thread 836 — but `railEntries()` rebuilds every entry on every tick and `Entry` is not memoised, so a settled message's tree is re-created while the next one streams, now 8.5× smaller. Unmeasured and stated: a plain inline span has no box, so its opacity animation is likelier to run on the main thread than the compositor. |

`app` is the baseline. A new prototype starts by copying the frame it changes,
not from nothing — so the thing being proposed is legible as a diff against
what exists.

Keeping it honest is the whole job. Every frame on `app` must match the code
in `src/ui/` and `src/runtime/`; when a design ships, the frame here becomes
what shipped. Read the implementation before trusting a frame — that is how
"design mode" survived here for months after select became the only pointer
tool. What the chrome is today: one 44px bar (brand lockup, project tabs, "+",
then threads toggle and zoom), the Pages rail at 248px, the Inspector rail at
300px with its elements and connections tabs, and the tool bar floating over
the bottom of the viewport. No mode switch. Play lives on the selection.

Explorations live until the work they decided is built. Making the decision is
not that moment: while a page is still being resolved, the rejected frames are
what the next session reads to see what was already argued and why, and a
component only the loser references dies with it. When the work ships, the
winner moves onto `app` as what shipped and the rest are deleted then. Git
history is the archive, and `git log --diff-filter=D --stat -- design/frames`
finds them.
