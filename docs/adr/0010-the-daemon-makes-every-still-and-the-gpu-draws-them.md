# The daemon makes every still, and the GPU draws them

A frame's still is made by the daemon's photo booth and drawn on the canvas by one WebGL2 layer. The canvas no longer photographs frames, and a frame showing its still has no element of its own in the page. Live documents stay what they were under [#107](https://github.com/liamvinberg/spool/issues/107): bounded, caused, and drawn by the browser above the GPU layer.

**The camera lives outside React.** One store holds it and is drawn once per animation frame. Every follower (labels, corners, arrows, walk tags, selection furniture, the agent hand, the picture layer) subscribes and writes its own styles in the same callback, and React hears the camera only when it comes to rest. A wheel tick used to re-render the whole canvas: 4 ms of main thread per pan tick and 6 to 9 ms per zoom tick on a 200-frame page, against an 8.3 ms frame at 120 Hz.

**The photo booth makes every still.** It is the pinned headless shell `spool shot` already used, launched with GPU rasterization, owned by the daemon in the CLI and the Mac app alike. One queue is fed by every project's file changes and by any frame read without a still, ordered by what open canvases have on screen, so pages nobody has open get stills too. A sitting loads the frame's own document, settles it with the shim's settle in an isolated world, and takes Chrome's own screenshot; a deadline enforced from the daemon bounds every sitting, and the browser closes 30 seconds after the queue drains. A still is the frame's first screen in its default scenario and the colour scheme its frames see, never where somebody left it, so one source hash has one picture. Compared with the canvas photographing its own frames, a 200-frame page is covered from nothing in 16 s instead of 25 s, a 754-frame project in about 140 s instead of 400, an edit reaches an open canvas in about 0.33 s instead of 0.43, and frames that draw with WebGL, `requestAnimationFrame`, entry animations or `showModal()` dialogs are no longer photographed blank.

**One WebGL2 layer draws the stills.** It sits behind the frame shells, ignores the pointer, and draws inside the camera store's callback, never in a loop of its own, so it cannot fall a frame behind the DOM. Each still is fetched and decoded once, kept on the GPU as a 64 px square in one texture array and as halvings streamed by the size it is drawn at, within a budget of two screens. A frame with a live or held document keeps its element, its own still and the cover fade of [#177](https://github.com/liamvinberg/spool/issues/177); a frame with no still yet keeps its placeholder. Hit testing was already by world position, so nothing about clicking changed. On a 1,000-frame overview this takes pans from 27 frames over 12 ms to under 1 and zooms from about 50 to about 8, and reload from 1.8 s to 0.8 s.

**Frames compile without stalling the daemon.** The booth compiles every frame of every project, so the compile had to stop blocking: a frame's Tailwind build runs on worker threads, a document is built and cached from one read of its inputs, and `shared/` imports are answered by the daemon with esbuild's own rules rather than a fresh resolver per import. The link graph keeps a stat witness per frame and checks every frame's bytes at least once a minute, so an unchanged project answers in about 30 ms at any size.

**Why not keep the canvas photographing as a fallback.** It would keep the whole errand machinery (borrowed frames, staleness, retries) alive for one case, a machine without the browser, and that case is already handled by fetching it, as `spool shot` always has. One codepath, as #107 asked.

**Why not draw labels on the GPU too.** It was built and measured. After the layer above, the label DOM costs about one slow frame on a 1,000-frame overview zoom, and moving it meant a second WebGL context per tab and a hand-made copy of the browser's text layout. Zero dropped frames is the wrong bar ([#81](https://github.com/liamvinberg/spool/issues/81)).

**Accepted trades.** Small frames look soft during a zoom until the camera rests. A live frame draws over a picture frame that overlaps it, whatever their order. Label widths may trail a zoom by a few frames and are exact at rest. The GPU layer holds tens of megabytes more than the old images did at mid zoom.

This supersedes the cover capture parts of [#107](https://github.com/liamvinberg/spool/issues/107): the canvas no longer borrows frames to photograph them, and a missing or stale picture is no longer a reason for a frame to mount.
