# Small edits in Figma: documented workflows

Research date: 2026-09-05. First-party Figma help and lessons, compared with the current `properties-map` combined prototype. The earlier [mapping report](visual-properties-mapping.md) primarily establishes layout and implementation constraints.

**Evidence boundary:** these sources establish what Figma supports and teaches. They do not establish usage frequency, shortcut recall, or a preference for dragging over typing. This pass includes no observed Figma sessions, interviews, telemetry, or timed comparison.

## A first-party worked sequence

Figma's responsive-card lesson connects selection, content editing, sizing, and checking the result. It has the learner duplicate a title, double-click to change its words, and use Command+R to rename the layer. It then groups the text in auto layout and sets a gap. When resizing the parent fails to resize the text as expected, the learner selects the parent, presses Enter to select its text children, sets horizontal Fill, and resizes again. The next steps add truncation, set different vertical behaviors for artwork and metadata, then test min/max limits. [Create a responsive card](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/18894664907287-Create-a-responsive-card-with-auto-layout-and-constraints)

The lesson teaches an iterative sequence: change, observe, adjust the relevant relationship, and check again. It authors a component; it does not establish occasional editors' habits.

## What Figma reports about people using the interface

Figma's 2024 UI3 retrospective describes reversals after its public beta. Floating panels cramped smaller canvases and disrupted ruler use; the team restored docked panels. Hiding blend modes behind an icon made people wait for a tooltip to read the setting. A clip-content dropdown added an extra click, so it returned to a checkbox. The team also restored simultaneous pixel values and sizing mode in auto-layout controls. This is Figma's account of feedback and evaluation, with no underlying dataset supplied. It supports testing the cost of concealed controls; it does not establish the best arrangement for spool. [Figma's UI3 retrospective](https://www.figma.com/blog/our-approach-to-designing-ui3/)

An accompanying first-person guide describes grouping width, height, resizing behavior, direction, alignment, and spacing to avoid repeated trips between panel sections. The author also describes applying an existing style or variable with the eyedropper directly from the canvas. This is a practitioner account published by the vendor, rather than independent comparative research. [Working in UI3](https://www.figma.com/blog/making-the-move-to-ui3-a-guide-to-figmas-next-chapter/)

Figma's guide for UX writers is closer to Liam's occasional-edit use case than a course about building a complete design system. Its author describes feedback from writers who found the tool intimidating, then teaches reaching nested text, editing it in the design, and letting an existing auto-layout component respond. It separately describes reusable local components and changing their main definition. These are different tasks even when they begin by selecting similar-looking objects. [The UX writer's guide](https://www.figma.com/best-practices/the-ux-writers-guide-to-figma/)

The 2020 auto-layout engineering retrospective is also relevant history. The team used an HTML prototype to investigate handles, drag behavior, and outlines. Later tests exposed confusion from simultaneously displaying similar constraints and resizing panels; the team combined the relevant controls. Its motivating example was changing a button label and otherwise having to repair the button, its siblings, and the surrounding dialog manually. This explains the importance of preserving layout relationships during a small edit. The article describes an earlier Figma version, not its complete present feature set. [Making auto layout](https://www.figma.com/blog/behind-the-feature-the-making-of-the-new-auto-layout/)

## Recommendation for spool's small-edit workflow

V1 should make existing, agent-authored UI easy to adjust. The category structure in `visual-inspector` remains a promising comparison, with its exact grouping provisional. Use stable, plainly named sections for the properties relevant to the selected element, and reveal additional detail where it is needed. A cleaner appearance should be assessed together with the steps required to finish an edit. Authoring a complete design from an empty canvas is a separate scope.

Three details deserve another prototype pass before production:

1. Let one spacing handle support dragging and direct numeric entry. Compare paired/all-side adjustment with the current modifier choice. Retain a visible way to choose exact values without making a familiar modifier mean two things during the same gesture.
2. Show measured size beside the sizing behavior, including Content and Fill. A mode describes a relationship; the number helps verify its current result.
3. Exercise the existing production selection and field controls in the proposed layout. The current three-target scene does not demonstrate nested navigation, field drafting, label scrubbing, or the relationship between editing and entering a live frame.

Use a short task sequence to judge the result: find a nested button, lengthen its label, adjust paired padding, move to its parent and change the gap, resize the parent to check reflow, then undo the spacing change. Separately test whether changing one label versus the shared definition has an understandable target. These are proposed checks, not completed observations of users.

This research supports a focused visual editor and identifies specific omissions in the first prototypes. It does not justify copying every Figma capability or claiming that the preferred arrangement has already been validated on real projects. The engineering questions are tracked in the [foundation audit](properties-foundation-audit.md).
